Read a battle as an RL loop

Article · Experiment-first wikiEdit source ↗

A battle is a sequence of decisions under partial information. The policy sees its legal observation, selects an action, receives a transition and reward, and updates its recurrent state. Training may change parameters; evaluation must not.

Separate state from observation

The simulator knows more than either player. An observation can include revealed moves and the player's own team while hiding the opponent's unrevealed choices. A spectator replay is not automatically a legal first-person training observation.

Before implementing anything, annotate one turn with what was known at decision time, what was revealed afterward, and what remained hidden. Check Metamon's project explanation for the reconstruction problem. Keep future information out of the policy input.

Follow one action

Write the legal moves and switches at a chosen turn. Predict what an action mask should exclude. Then inspect the selected action, damage or status outcome, reward, and termination signal. A shaped reward is a training signal; winning the battle is an evaluation outcome. They can disagree.

An illegal action, disconnect, crash, or timeout needs an explicit outcome rule in the locked protocol. Quietly dropping it changes the measured policy.

Watch and predict

Watch · Internet required. Open the Metamon Gen 1 OU demonstration. Pause before a switch. List two plausible opponent actions and say what evidence favors each. The hosted clip is a behavior example, not proof that this repository reproduces it.

Play · Internet required. Use Arthur Juliani's RL playground. Change epsilon while holding the reward map fixed. Predict how the action probabilities change. A visible gridworld has much less hidden information than a battle, so do not carry its assumptions over unchanged.

Trace a learning target

A return uses future rewards along an episode. A temporal-difference target also uses a value estimate at a later state. Bootstrap error, limited observations, and distribution shift can affect that estimate.

Try · Internet required. Play the opening animation in The paths perspective on value learning. Compare how Monte Carlo and temporal-difference estimates use intersecting paths. Then sketch one possible battle trajectory. State where the analogy fails under hidden information.

Write a hypothesis before choosing one policy improvement.
